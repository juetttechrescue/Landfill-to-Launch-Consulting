The part nobody prepares you for

Getting set up is not the hard part. What comes after is.

Landing your website and your first few customers is work, but it is not what stops people. The stretch that stops people is the one after that: you have traction, you are addressing a real gap, but the partnerships and infrastructure that make it compound are not signed yet.

That waiting period is where most people stall. It is also where the money is won or lost, because everything comes down to how you handle the waste you collect. I have been through it, and I help you move through it deliberately instead of by guessing.

I am a junior at CU Boulder studying economics and political science, concentrated in market research. My coursework in quantitative research methods, macroeconomics and microeconomics, law and society, and geographic sustainability and futures gave me the analytical framework I now apply to this industry: how to size a market, read demand, and find the segment no one is serving.

Applying that framework to electronic waste surfaced the opportunity. E-waste is the fastest-growing solid waste stream on earth, and demand is compounding as household device ownership rises. Almost none of that growth is being met where residents and small businesses live: e-waste is only being addressed head-on at the enterprise scale. Options exist for the community stream, but none of them are straightforward, convenient, or affordable. General haulers and recycling facilities pick up e-waste as a side offering rather than a specialty, so pricing fluctuates and runs high, and data security and device refurbishment get little to no attention, since neither is core to their business. Add the regulatory picture, no federal blanket ban on landfilling electronics and requirements that shift state to state, and you get a market that is large, growing, and structurally underserved.

What I want is for you to come out of this with something that carries weight: not a line you have to explain away, but a business you actually ran. Revenue you earned, customers you acquired, and decisions you made yourself. That is what people remember you for, on an application and in the room.

The case study

Juett Tech Rescue: the model, proven

Landfill to Launch is not theory. It is built on a business I started, run, and grew in a real market. Juett Tech Rescue is the test case that shows the playbook works.

I built and run Juett Tech Rescue LLC, the first electronic-waste management service in Boulder County, Colorado. The business makes e-waste recycling affordable and convenient through a flat-fee, door-to-door model built to remove the barriers that keep people from recycling electronics.

Eco-Cycle, one of the nation's largest and oldest nonprofit recycling facilities, reached out to me directly to explore a partnership. When the top local processor calls you, that is real demand.
